## PRIMARY ANSWER — the goal-setting chain and its documents

The VOC was a chartered company: the **States-General** issued the founding **octrooi
(charter) of 20 March 1602**, and *within* the Company the goals of expeditions were set by a
tiered chain of issuing bodies and document genres.

**1. The charter (octrooi, 1602) — the enabling grant.**
The States-General granted the Company a monopoly and, overseas, the right to act almost as a
sovereign. The Nationaal Archief's own archive introduction states the charter "allowed the
Company far reaching rights overseas: the VOC could build forts in Asia; employ soldiers;
conclude treaties with Asian rulers; and appoint judges."
**Confidence: HIGH** (source: https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/sites/default/files/afbeeldingen/toegangen/NL-HaNA_1.04.02_introduction-VOC.pdf , fetched/05, L764). These powers are the legal basis for every later expedition goal (trade, fortification, war, diplomacy, governance).

**2. The Heren XVII — the policy-setting board, by resolution.**
The charter itself vests expedition-level decisions in the central board. Article III of the
octrooi: the College "zal te zamen komen, om te resolveren, wanneer men zal equiperen, met
hoeveel schepen, waar men die zal zenden, ende andere dingen, den handel betreffende" — *will
assemble to resolve when to fit out, with how many ships, where to send them, and other matters
concerning the trade.*
**Confidence: HIGH** (source: https://www.vocsite.nl/geschiedenis/octrooi1602/ , fetched/01, Art. III). So the **resolutions of the Heren XVII** are the charter-mandated instrument for assigning a fleet's size, destination and trade goals.

**3. Sealed sailing instructions to fleet admirals (early period).**
Before a fixed Asian headquarters existed, the Heren XVII handed an outbound admiral **secret
written instructions, opened only after leaving port.** For the first VOC fleet (Dec 1603),
admiral Steven van der Hagen "was sent to sea, with secret instructions only to be opened after
leaving port ... the 'Heren XVII' (the directors) ordered him to fight the Spanish and
Portuguese." **Confidence: MEDIUM** (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_van_der_Hagen , fetched/08; tertiary, used qualitatively — corroborated by the NA charter-powers grant and octrooi Art. III). The admiral of the outbound fleet initially held "the supreme command in Asia and all the Company's employees were subject to him" (NA introduction, fetched/05, L770-71, **Confidence: HIGH**).

**4. The Governor-General and Council at Batavia — instructions in theatre.**
From 1609 the directors placed supreme command in Asia in a **Governor-General assisted by a
Raad van Indië**; after 1619 Batavia became the seat of this "Supreme Government" and the
"rendez-vous for the Company's shipping traffic." From there the high government issued
instructions to expeditions, garrisons and officers within Asia.
**Confidence: HIGH** (source: NA introduction, fetched/05, L775-780).

**5. The generale missiven — the reporting / goal-feedback loop.**
The Governor-General and Council reported back to the Heren XVII in the annual **generale
missive**, "in which the Governor-General and Council reported to the Heren XVII about the
state of Company business in Asia," and compiled the **generale eis van Indië** (the general
order stating money, goods, ships and crews needed), which "served as a guideline for
decision-making" in the Heren XVII's sessions.
**Confidence: HIGH** (source: NA introduction, fetched/05, L795-803). Coen's 6 May 1621 Banda dispatch (Sub-Q2) is exactly such a governor's missive to the Heren XVII.

**Categories of assigned goals, 1602–1700** (each traceable to the above genres): securing and
defending the **spice monopoly** (the NA introduction frames the whole Asian effort around
"the world monopoly on fine spices," fetched/05, L107); **building and garrisoning forts**;
**making treaties/alliances** with Asian rulers and waging war on Iberian rivals; **founding
and governing settlements** (Batavia 1619; the Cape refreshment post 1652, Sub-Q3); and
**planting colonies / depopulating-and-resettling** conquered ground (Banda 1621, Sub-Q2).
**Confidence: HIGH** for the categories as a set (sources: NA introduction fetched/05; octrooi
fetched/01; the two sub-question primaries below).

## Dutch-primary sub-question 1 — the 1602 octrooi article on the board of seventeen

**Located source:** the 20 March 1602 octrooi, Article II, transcribed at
https://www.vocsite.nl/geschiedenis/octrooi1602/ (fetched/01-vocsite-octrooi1602.html).

**Dutch text (Article II), as found:**
> "Dat, zoo dikwils als het van nooden zyn zal een generale Vergadering ofte Collegie uit de
> voorschreve kameren te houden, die gehouden zal werden van **17 persoonen, daar in uit de
> kamer van Amsterdam zullen compareren 8, uit Zeeland 4, uit de Maaze 2, ende van gelyken uit
> Noordholland 2; wel verstaande, dat de zeventiende persoon by beurten van die van Zeeland,
> Maaze ende Noordholland, zal werden in de Vergadering gebragt by de meeste stemmen**; van
> welke persoonen alle zaaken, deze vereenigde Compagnien aangaande, zullen verhandelt worden."

**My translation:**
> "That, as often as it shall be necessary to hold a general Assembly or College from the
> aforesaid chambers, it shall consist of **17 persons, in which from the chamber of Amsterdam
> 8 shall appear, from Zeeland 4, from the Maas 2, and likewise from North-Holland 2; it being
> understood that the seventeenth person shall, by turns, be brought into the Assembly from
> those of Zeeland, the Maas and North-Holland, by the most votes**; by which persons all
> matters concerning these united Companies shall be dealt with."

**Answer.**
- **Per-chamber allocation of the 17 seats:** Amsterdam **8**, Zeeland **4**, the Maas (Maaze /
  Rotterdam-Delft grouping) **2**, North-Holland (Hoorn-Enkhuizen grouping) **2** — totalling
  **16** fixed seats.
- **Rule for the seventeenth seat:** the 17th man is **NOT** an Amsterdam seat. He is supplied
  **"by beurten" (by rotation/in turns) among Zeeland, the Maas and North-Holland**, and is
  "in de Vergadering gebragt by de meeste stemmen" (brought into the assembly by the most
  votes). The device deliberately keeps Amsterdam — which holds 8 of 17 — short of a standing
  majority. **Confidence: HIGH** (source: fetched/01; corroborated, in the later six-chamber phrasing, by https://www.vocsite.nl/geschiedenis/organisatie/ , fetched/06: "Amsterdam ... acht afgevaardigden, Zeeland vier en de overige kleinere kamers ieder één. Het 17e lid kwam bij toerbeurt uit Zeeland of de [kleinere kamers]").

*(Note on chamber names: the 1602 charter groups the smaller chambers as "de Maaze" and
"Noordholland/Westvriesland." These are the later separate chambers Rotterdam + Delft, and
Hoorn + Enkhuizen. Standard six-chamber summaries therefore render the same allocation as
Amsterdam 8, Zeeland 4, and one each from Rotterdam, Delft, Hoorn, Enkhuizen, with a rotating
17th — arithmetically identical.)*

## Dutch-primary sub-question 2 — Coen's Banda dispatch of 6 May 1621

**Located source:** J.P. Coen to the Heren XVII, 6 May 1621, printed in H.T. Colenbrander (ed.),
*Jan Pietersz. Coen, bescheiden omtrent zijn bedrijf in Indië*, vol. 1, pp. 627-639 (the
edition of NA archief 1.04.02 inv. 1073). Full text:
https://archive.org/stream/janpieterszcoenb01coen/janpieterszcoenb01coen_djvu.txt
(fetched/04-coen-bescheiden-v1-djvu.txt). The letter closes "Int schip Nieuw Hollandia ...
op 't eylandt Nera, in Banda, adi 6 Mayo anno 1621. ... J. P. COEN."

### (a) Coen's own losses in the hill engagement, and any named officer

The assault on the heights of Lontor began **11 March 1621**. The named vanguard officer was
**Cappitejn Vogel (a free-burgher of Amboina)**, who led 50 picked soldiers up the mountain
and met fierce resistance but was *not* reported killed. The Dutch loss in the hill march
itself is given as one dead and a handful wounded:

> "... dan dat op de voorgangers van een verlooren troupe een scherge met ontrent 10 a 20
> mosquetten gedaen wierden, **waerdoor een van d'onse doot geschoten is ende 4 a 5 gequest
> zijn.**" (fetched/04, L31276-78)
>
> *"... except that on the vanguard of a forlorn troop a volley of about 10 to 20 muskets was
> fired, whereby **one of ours was shot dead and 4 to 5 were wounded.**"*

For the campaign as a whole, Coen totals his own losses:

> "In alle de voorsz. rescontres hebben **5 mannen verlooren ende ontrent 20 gequeste**
> gecregen." (fetched/04, L31320-21)
>
> *"In all the aforesaid encounters we lost **5 men and got about 20 wounded.**"*

**Answer (a):** In the hill engagement itself — **1 killed and 4–5 wounded** of Coen's own
force; **no named officer was lost** (the named captain, Vogel, led the charge and survived).
Across all the encounters of the conquest, Coen's own total was **5 killed and about 20
wounded.** **Confidence: HIGH** (source: fetched/04, Colenbrander ed.).

### (b) What the ~1,200 figure actually refers to

Just before his signature Coen writes:

> "**In alles hebben ontrent 1200 zielen van haer becomen; daerenboven sijn noch verscheyde
> andere doot gebleven.**" (fetched/04, L31790-91)
>
> *"**In all we have obtained about 1,200 souls from them; besides these, various others were
> killed.**"*

**Answer (b):** The ~1,200 is **NOT a death toll.** It is the number of **Bandanese taken
alive / captured** ("becomen") and **deported as prisoners** — Coen ships them to Jacatra
(Batavia) on the *Dragon* (the same dispatch sends "789 zielen ... 287 mannen, 256 vrouwen,
246 kinderen" by the *Dragon*, fetched/04, L31423). Coen's own grammar separates the captured
from the dead ("daerenboven ... doot gebleven"). His later letter of 16 Nov 1621 confirms the
referent: "**van Slamma gelicht 1200 sielen**" (lifted 1,200 souls from Slamma) is set against
"**Ontrent 2500 ... vergaen**" (about 2,500 perished by hunger/misery and the sword) — two
different categories (fetched/04, L31953-55). **Confidence: HIGH** (source: fetched/04;
referent independently corroborated by modern scholarship, which likewise counts Banda's
killed and enslaved as separate categories: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_conquest_of_the_Banda_Islands ).

So: **~1,200 = the Bandanese captured alive and deported** (chiefly the Slamma population),
distinct from the much larger number Coen reports as dead.

## Dutch-primary sub-question 3 — Van Riebeeck's Daghregister heading, Dec 1651

**Located source:** *Daghregister, gehouden door Jan Anthonisz. van Riebeeck, Deel I,
1651-1655* (the critical edition of NA archief 1.04.02 inv. 1188), opening register on p. 1,
via DBNL: https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/rieb001dagh01_01/rieb001dagh01_01_0004.php
(fetched/03-dbnl-riebeeck-daghregister.html).

**Dutch text (the register heading), as found:**
> "**Daghregister, gehouden bij den oppercoopman Jan Anthonisz. van Riebeeck, vertrocken per
> de schepen Drommedaris, Reijger ende Goede Hoope voor opperhooft naer Cabo de boä Esperance,
> in dienst van de Generale Vereenighde Nederlantsche G'octroyeerde Oostindische Comp. uijt
> Texel, van de Camer Amsterdam.**"

**My translation:**
> "Day-register, kept by the chief merchant Jan Anthonisz. van Riebeeck, departed with the
> ships *Drommedaris, Reijger* and *Goede Hoope* as commander/head toward the Cape of Good
> Hope, in the service of the General United Netherlands Chartered East India Company, out of
> Texel, of the Chamber of Amsterdam."

**Answer.**
- **(a) The three ships:** ***Drommedaris*, *Reijger*, and *Goede Hoope*** (Goede Hoop).
- **(b) Van Riebeeck's stated rank/role:** rank **oppercoopman** (chief/senior merchant),
  sailing **"voor opperhooft"** — i.e. as the commander / chief of the voyage and the
  intended head of the post.
- **(c) Port and chamber:** he sailed **out of Texel** ("uijt Texel") and **under the Chamber
  of Amsterdam** ("van de Camer Amsterdam").

**Confidence: HIGH** (source: fetched/03, DBNL critical edition of the Daghregister).
