## PART A — PRIMARY question: what goals were assigned to VOC expeditions, 1602–1700, and through which issuing documents

### A.1 The charter (octrooi) — the founding mandate

The 1602 octrooi, granted by the States-General at The Hague on 20 March 1602
and reproduced by François Valentijn (1724), is the root document. Its **Article
III** is the charter-level statement of what the central board decides about
every expedition:

> "'t Voorschreve Collegie, als het beschreven zal worden, zal te zamen komen,
> om te resolveren, **wanneer men zal equiperen, met hoeveel schepen, waar men
> die zal zenden, ende andere dingen, den handel betreffende**: ende zullen de
> resolutien van 't voorschreven Collegie by de voorschreve kameren van
> Amsterdam, Zeeland, Maaze ende Noordholland geeffectueert, ende in 't werk
> gestelt worden."

My translation: "The aforesaid College [the Seventeen], when convened, shall
assemble to resolve **when one shall fit out [an expedition], with how many
ships, where one shall send them, and other matters concerning trade**; and the
resolutions of the aforesaid College shall be carried out and put into effect by
the aforesaid chambers of Amsterdam, Zeeland, the Maze and North Holland."

So the charter itself assigns the *goal-setting* power (timing, fleet size,
destination, "other things concerning trade") to the Heren XVII, with the
**chambers** executing — equipping and dispatching the actual ships.
**Confidence: HIGH** (source: https://www.vocsite.nl/geschiedenis/octrooi1602/).

### A.2 Who issued goals to expeditions, and through which documents

The goal-issuing flowed top-down and is documented in a layered set of
instruments:

1. **Heren XVII resolutions** — the central board met two to three times a year
   (six years in Amsterdam, then two in Middelburg, in an eight-year cycle) and
   resolved policy; the chamber directors "waren verantwoordelijk voor het
   uitvoeren van het beleid van de Heren XVII" (were responsible for executing
   the Heren XVII's policy). **Confidence: HIGH**
   (source: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heren_XVII).

2. **Chamber-level dispatch / sailing instructions** — the chambers (from whose
   ports the voyages were organised: "de bewindhebbers van de afzonderlijke
   kamers, waarvandaan de zeereizen georganiseerd werden") fitted out and sent
   the ships and gave the commander his written instruction. The Van Riebeeck
   case (Part C, sub-Q3) is a worked example: he sailed under a written
   instruction from the Heren XVII to build a fort and refreshment station at the
   Cape. **Confidence: HIGH**
   (sources: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heren_XVII ;
   https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/beleven/onderwijs/bronnenbox/hoe-ging-het-verder-op-banda).

3. **Reporting and tasking back from Asia — the Generale Missiven.** From Batavia,
   the Governor-General and Council reported to the Heren XVII in annual "general
   letters": "De generale missiven zijn de jaarlijkse brieven waarin
   Gouverneur-Generaal en Raden van de Verenigde Oost-Indische Compagnie ...
   verslag deden" — covering the period 1610–1761. These both reported on, and
   shaped, what local expeditions and fleets were ordered to do (trade, war,
   garrisons, monopoly enforcement). **Confidence: HIGH**
   (source: https://resources.huygens.knaw.nl/vocgeneralemissiven).

4. **Journals / voyage reports mandated by rule.** In 1617 the VOC required a
   journal (journaal) to be kept and all voyage accounts (reisverslagen) handed
   in on return; in 1643 it ordered that all octrooi territories be systematically
   described. "In 1617 bepaalde de VOC dat er een journaal moest worden
   bijgehouden en dat na terugkomst alle reisverslagen moesten worden ingeleverd
   ... In 1643 werd bepaald dat alle octrooigebieden systematisch moesten worden
   beschreven." Van Riebeeck's Daghregister (sub-Q3) is exactly such a mandated
   journal. **Confidence: HIGH** (source: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heren_XVII).

5. **Secret instructions for commissarissen-generaal** and a parallel series of
   *secrete* resolutions/instructions existed alongside the ordinary ones, kept
   by the advocaat (secretary). **Confidence: MEDIUM**
   (source: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heren_XVII — the page lists "secreete
   boeken" and secret stukken kept by the advocaat).

### A.3 The substantive goals assigned, 1602–1700 (illustrative, sourced)

- **Trade and monopoly enforcement in spices** (cloves, nutmeg, mace) in the East
  Indies — pursued by treaty and force, culminating in conquests such as Makassar.
  **Confidence: MEDIUM** (source, general/secondary:
  https://www.huygens.knaw.nl/resources/generale-missiven-van-gouverneurs-generaal-en-raden-aan-heren-xvii-der-verenigde-oostindische-compagnie/).
- **A logistics/refreshment node at the Cape** — fort, garden, water, livestock
  trade with the Khoi, explicitly *not* a colony, and no enslaving of locals.
  **Confidence: HIGH** for the goal-set
  (source: https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/beleven/onderwijs/bronnenbox/hoe-ging-het-verder-op-banda
  and the Daghregister heading itself, Part B).
- **Subjugation/depopulation of Banda to secure the nutmeg monopoly** — the 6 May
  1621 dispatch (Part B) is itself the report of an expedition executing this goal
  ("Om een eynd van den oorloch in Banda te becomen, ende t' eenenmael meester te
  blyven" — to end the war in Banda and remain wholly master). **Confidence: HIGH**
  (source: https://archive.org/details/janpieterszcoenb01coen).

## PART B — Dutch-primary sub-answers

### Sub-Q1 — 1602 octrooi: allocation of the seventeen seats and the seventeenth-seat rule

**Primary source fetched and translated:** the full text of the 1602 octrooi,
**Article II**, in Valentijn's transcription.
URL: https://www.vocsite.nl/geschiedenis/octrooi1602/ (artifact: `fetched/01-octrooi1602-vocsite.md`).

Verbatim Dutch (Article II):

> "Dat, zoo dikwils als het van nooden zyn zal een generale Vergadering ofte
> Collegie uit de voorschreve kameren te houden, die gehouden zal werden van **17
> persoonen**, daar in **uit de kamer van Amsterdam zullen compareren 8, uit
> Zeeland 4, uit de Maaze 2, ende van gelyken uit Noordholland 2**; wel
> verstaande, **dat de zeventiende persoon by beurten van die van Zeeland, Maaze
> ende Noordholland, zal werden in de Vergadering gebragt by de meeste stemmen**;
> van welke persoonen alle zaaken, deze vereenigde Compagnien aangaande, zullen
> verhandelt worden."

My translation: "That, as often as it shall be necessary to hold a general
Meeting or College out of the aforesaid chambers, it shall be held by **17
persons**, of whom **from the chamber of Amsterdam 8 shall appear, from Zeeland
4, from the Maze 2, and likewise from North Holland 2**; it being understood
**that the seventeenth person shall be brought into the Meeting by turns by those
of Zeeland, the Maze and North Holland, by majority of votes**; by which persons
all matters concerning these united Companies shall be transacted."

**Answer:**
- Per-chamber allocation of the seventeen: **Amsterdam 8, Zeeland 4, the Maze
  (Delft + Rotterdam) 2, North Holland (Hoorn + Enkhuizen) 2** — that is 16 fixed
  seats.
- The **seventeenth** seat is **not Amsterdam's**: it is supplied **by rotation
  ("by beurten") among Zeeland, the Maze and North Holland**, decided by majority
  vote. The deliberate effect was to deny Amsterdam an outright majority.

**Confidence: HIGH** (source: https://www.vocsite.nl/geschiedenis/octrooi1602/).
Corroboration: https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heren_XVII — "Het zeventiende lid
werd bij toerbeurt afgevaardigd door Zeeland en een van de vier kleine kamers.
Daarmee werd voorkomen dat Amsterdam een absolute meerderheid kreeg."

Note on provenance: this is Valentijn's 1724 re-spelled transcription of the 1602
charter (he added the article numbers); the substance of the allocation is
independently confirmed by the Dutch Wikipedia article on the Heren XVII.

### Sub-Q2 — Coen's 6 May 1621 dispatch: own losses in the hill engagement, and what the ~1,200 figure means

**Primary source fetched and translated:** Coen's letter of "6 Mayo anno 1621,"
written from "het schip Nieuw Hollandia ... voor 't fort Nassouw op 't eylandt
Nera, in Banda," addressed to the Bewindhebbers (Heren XVII), as published in
H.T. Colenbrander (ed.), *Jan Pietersz. Coen: Bescheiden omtrent zijn bedrijf in
Indië*, Deel I (Nijhoff, 1919), **pp. 638–639**.
URL: https://archive.org/details/janpieterszcoenb01coen (full text:
https://archive.org/stream/janpieterszcoenb01coen/janpieterszcoenb01coen_djvu.txt ;
artifact: `fetched/06-coen-bescheiden-vol1-6mei1621.md`).
This is the same letter the Nationaal Archief catalogues as **1.04.02, inv.nr.
1073** (confirmed: https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/beleven/onderwijs/bronnenbox/hoe-ging-het-verder-op-banda).

Verbatim Early-Modern-Dutch (OCR of original spelling; the hill engagement of
"den 2en deser" = 2 May 1621):

> "Naer 't schrijven vant voorsz., hebben den 2en deser een troupe van 80
> musquettiers ende 2 compagnien van 70 koppen yder tot een retraite off
> achterhoude van d'andre op 't geberchte gesonden ... Sy wierden weder
> teruggedreven, doch d'onse afftreckende heeft den vyandt haer langs de rugge
> vant geberchte (die tot haeren voordeel smal was) soo cort gevolcht, dat veel
> ongeregelde matrosen met disordre doorliepen ... **In dese rescontre hebben 35
> gequesten ende 9 dooden gecregen, daeronder cappiteyn de Ros met sijn
> vendrich.**"

> "... **In alles hebben ontrent 1200 zielen van haer becomen; daerenboven sijn
> noch verscheyde andere doot gebleven.**"

My translation:
- "After the writing of the above, on the 2nd of this month we sent a troop of 80
  musketeers and 2 companies of 70 men each, as a rearguard for the others, up
  into the mountains ... They [the enemy] were driven back again, but as our men
  withdrew, the enemy followed them so closely along the ridge of the mountain
  (which to their advantage was narrow) that many disorderly sailors ran on in
  confusion ... **In this encounter we got 35 wounded and 9 dead, among them
  Captain de Ros with his ensign [flag-bearer].**"
- "... **In all we have got [taken/obtained] about 1,200 souls from them; besides
  which, several others have been killed.**"

**Answer:**
- **(a) Coen's own losses in the hill engagement (2 May 1621): 35 wounded and 9
  dead.** The named officer lost is **Captain de Ros**, "met sijn vendrich" —
  together with his ensign / flag-bearer (vaandrig).
- **(b) The ~1,200 figure refers to ~1,200 Bandanese "souls" the Dutch
  *obtained/took* ("becomen")** — i.e. brought into their power (captured /
  taken), reported as a *cumulative* total ("in alles" = in all) for the whole
  Banda action, **not** the casualty count of the 2 May hill fight. Coen states
  the killed are an *additional* number ("daerenboven sijn noch verscheyde andere
  doot gebleven"). So the 1,200 is the running total of Bandanese taken, kept
  separate from the dead.

CONTRADICTION FLAGGED: the Nationaal Archief's educational *Hertaling* glosses
this line as "(red.: zijn circa 1200 man overleden)" — i.e. ~1,200 *killed*. That
is an editorial interpretation. The literal verb in Coen's original is
**"becomen"** (= to get/obtain/take), the same word Coen uses elsewhere for
"verseeckerder staet te becomen" (to gain a more secure position,
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_conquest_of_the_Banda_Islands), and the dead
are explicitly counted *in addition*. I report the literal primary reading
(≈1,200 taken/obtained) and note the NA "killed" gloss as a divergence.
**Confidence: HIGH** for the verbatim text and the 35/9/de-Ros figures; **HIGH**
that the 1,200 is the obtained/taken total in the original wording; **MEDIUM** on
adjudicating the captured-vs-killed historiographical debate
(sources: https://archive.org/details/janpieterszcoenb01coen ;
https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/beleven/onderwijs/bronnenbox/hoe-ging-het-verder-op-banda).

### Sub-Q3 — Van Riebeeck's Daghregister, December 1651 outbound voyage: opening register heading

**Primary source fetched and translated:** Jan van Riebeeck, *Daghregister*, Deel
1 (1651–1655), DBNL edition (1952), the opening register heading.
URL: https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/rieb001dagh01_01/rieb001dagh01_01_0004.php
(artifact: `fetched/02-vanriebeeck-daghregister-dbnl.md`); the December 1651
departure entries are at
https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/rieb001dagh01_01/rieb001dagh01_01_0005.php
(artifact: `fetched/03-vanriebeeck-daghregister-entries.md`).

Verbatim Dutch (the register heading):

> "Daghregister, gehouden bij den **oppercoopman Jan Anthonisz. van Riebeeck**,
> vertrocken per de schepen **Drommedaris, Reijger ende Goede Hoope voor
> opperhooft** naer **Cabo de boä Esperance**, in dienst van de Generale
> Vereenighde Nederlantsche G'octroyeerde Oostindische Comp. **uijt Texel, van de
> Camer Amsterdam.**"

My translation: "Journal kept by the **upper-merchant [opperkoopman] Jan
Anthonisz. van Riebeeck**, departed with the ships **Drommedaris, Reijger and
Goede Hoope as chief [opperhoofd / commander]** for the **Cape of Good Hope
[Cabo de boa Esperança]**, in the service of the General United Netherlands
Chartered East India Company, **from Texel, of the Chamber of Amsterdam.**"

The voyage entries confirm the December 1651 date: the journal opens "December
anno 1651, In 't schip den drommedaris," and on "Sondagh, 24 do." (Sunday 24
December) the three ships "den Drommedaris, Reyger ende Hoope" sailed out of
Texel into the sea.

**Answer:**
- **(a) The three ships:** **Drommedaris** (the flagship), **Reijger** (a fluit),
  and **Goede Hoope** (a jacht).
- **(b) Van Riebeeck's stated rank/role:** his rank is **oppercoopman
  (upper-merchant)**, and his shipboard role on the voyage is **voor opperhooft —
  as chief / commander** of the small fleet. (DBNL's editor notes his Cape
  appointment carried the title "Koopman en Opperhoofd" with an opperkoopman's
  salary, which is why the heading styles him oppercoopman.)
- **(c) Port and chamber:** he sailed **from Texel**, under the **Chamber of
  Amsterdam (Camer Amsterdam).**

**Confidence: HIGH** (source: https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/rieb001dagh01_01/rieb001dagh01_01_0004.php ;
date corroborated at https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/rieb001dagh01_01/rieb001dagh01_01_0005.php).
Provenance note: the manuscript shelfmark inv.nr. 1188 / folio 189 in the question
was matched by *content* (the DBNL edition reproduces this exact register heading
and the December 1651 entries); I did not separately fetch a folio-189 scan, so the
precise folio number is **taken from the question, not re-verified against a
manuscript image** — flagged.
