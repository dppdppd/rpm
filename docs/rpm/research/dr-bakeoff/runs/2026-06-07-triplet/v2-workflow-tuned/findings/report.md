# VOC expedition goal-setting and its issuing documents, 1602–1700

**Research question.** What goals did the chambers, the Heren XVII, the High
Government at Batavia, and senior officers assign to VOC (Dutch East India
Company) expeditions between 1602 and 1700, and through which issuing
documents were those goals communicated?

**How to read this report.** Every load-bearing statement carries a confidence
tag (HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW) and a source URL. Where the adversarial review found a
claim genuinely ambiguous, both readings are presented side by side and
labelled *contested* — this is a finding, not a gap. Where a rival reading won
outright, the rival is asserted here and the original is recorded only in
`../validation/refuted.md`. Claims that could not be verified against a located
source are confined to the final section and are never asserted as fact.

**Bottom line.** VOC expedition goals were set in a layered, document-driven
system. The 1602 charter (octrooi) created the legal envelope and the governing
board; the Heren XVII fixed the annual scale of men, ships, money, and goods in
their autumn resolutions and wrote the fleet instructions; the High Government
at Batavia translated Asian needs into demand documents (the eisen) and reported
back through the Generale Missiven. Several popular characterisations of this
system overstate what the founding charter actually granted and mislabel the
demand documents; those are flagged below.

## 1. The founding envelope: the 1602 charter (octrooi)

The States General granted the VOC charter at The Hague on **20 March 1602**
("Gegeven onder onzen Zegel ... in 's Gravenhage den 20den Maart des jaars
1602"). **[HIGH** — primary text, https://nl.wikisource.org/wiki/Octrooi_van_de_VOC ;
corroborated at https://www.vocsite.nl/geschiedenis/octrooi1602/ **]**

The charter is the document from which expedition authority ultimately derives,
but the exact powers it grants are narrower than the standard secondary summary
suggests. Two articles carry the load:

- **Monopoly grant (Article XXXIV).** For 21 years from 1602, no one from the
  United Provinces other than the Company may sail "Beoosten de kaap de Bonne
  Esperance, ofte door de straat van Magellanes" (east of the Cape of Good Hope,
  or through the Strait of Magellan), on pain of forfeiture of ships and goods.
  **[HIGH** — https://nl.wikisource.org/wiki/Octrooi_van_de_VOC **]**

- **Sovereign-type powers (Article XXXV).** East of the Cape and in/through the
  Strait of Magellan, the Company may "met de Princen ende Potentaten
  verbintenis te maaken, ende contracten op den naam van de Staaten Generaal"
  (make alliances and contracts with princes and potentates in the name of the
  States General), build "fortressen ende verzekertheden" (forts and
  strongholds), and appoint "gouverneurs, volk van oorlog, ende officiers van
  Justitie" (governors, men of war, and officers of justice) — all of whom swear
  an oath of fidelity to the States General and to the Company.
  **[HIGH** — https://nl.wikisource.org/wiki/Octrooi_van_de_VOC **]**

The duration clause (Article VII) independently confirms the company "zal
beginnen ... met dezen jaare 1602, ende zal geduuren tot den tyd van 21 jaaren."
**[HIGH** — https://nl.wikisource.org/wiki/Octrooi_van_de_VOC **]**

### Contested: what the 1602 octrooi actually grants

> The founding date (20 March 1602), the 21-year monopoly between the Cape of
> Good Hope and the Strait of Magellan, and the powers to build forts, make
> treaties, wage war, and appoint judicial officers are all confirmed verbatim in
> the charter. **The two further powers commonly attributed to the charter —
> "striking coins" and "establishing colonies" — do not appear anywhere in the
> 46-article text.** The strings *munt*, *colonie*, *coloni*, *volkplant*, and
> "geld/penningen slaan" return zero matches across the full transcription.
> These are standard secondary-source glosses (present, for example, in the
> Wikipedia article) that describe later VOC practice rather than the founding
> grant; the VOC's own coin-striking began only in the 1640s under separate
> authority.

This is labelled contested/ambiguous because the broad characterisation is
defensible as a description of the company *as an institution*, while the
literal charter text supports only the narrower reading.

- **Reading A (original, secondary summary), MEDIUM:** The 1602 octrooi conferred
  quasi-sovereign powers including building forts, making treaties, waging war,
  appointing judicial officers, **striking coins, and establishing colonies.**
  Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_India_Company

- **Reading B (primary-text reading), HIGH for what it asserts:** Articles XXXIV–XXXV
  grant the 21-year monopoly, fort-building, treaty-making in the name of the
  States General, appointment of governors / troops / justice officers, and
  troop-maintenance — and **do not** grant coin-striking or colony-founding.
  Source: https://nl.wikisource.org/wiki/Octrooi_van_de_VOC

## 2. The governing board: composition of the Heren XVII

The board that decided whether, when, and with how many ships to equip
expeditions is set up in the article the Valentijn/Wikisource transcription
numbers **Article II**. Translating it directly:

> "as often as it shall be necessary to hold a general meeting or College from
> the aforesaid chambers, it shall be held by 17 persons, among whom from the
> chamber of Amsterdam shall appear 8, from Zeeland 4, from the Maas 2, and
> likewise from Noord-Holland 2; it being understood that the seventeenth person
> shall be brought into the meeting **by turns** by those of Zeeland, the Maas,
> and Noord-Holland, **by majority vote**."
> ("die gehouden zal werden van 17 persoonen, daar in uit de kamer van Amsterdam
> zullen compareren 8, uit Zeeland 4, uit de Maaze 2, ende van gelyken uit
> Noordholland 2; wel verstaande, dat de zeventiende persoon by beurten van die
> van Zeeland, Maaze ende Noordholland, zal werden in de Vergadering gebragt by
> de meeste stemmen")
> **[HIGH** — identical text in two independent transcriptions:
> https://nl.wikisource.org/wiki/Octrooi_van_de_VOC and
> https://www.vocsite.nl/geschiedenis/octrooi1602/ **]**

> **Sub-question 1 — answer.** Per-chamber allocation of the seventeen seats:
> **Amsterdam 8, Zeeland 4, the Maas (de Maaze) 2, Noord-Holland 2** — that is
> 16 fixed seats. The **seventeenth seat rotates** among Zeeland, the Maas, and
> Noord-Holland: it is filled "by turns" (by beurten) by those three bodies,
> decided **by majority vote** ("by de meeste stemmen"). Note that the seat is
> *not* reserved to Zeeland; all three minor groupings share it in rotation.

### Contested: six named cities vs. four voting blocs

The mature VOC had six physical chambers (Amsterdam, Zeeland, Enkhuizen, Hoorn,
Delft, Rotterdam), and that six-chamber framing is correct for the institution.
But the charter article above allocates seats to **four voting blocs**, not six
cities: the two Maas cities (Rotterdam + Delft) share the "de Maaze" pair of
seats, and the two Noorderkwartier cities (Enkhuizen + Hoorn) share the
"Noordholland" pair.

- **Reading A (six individual cities), MEDIUM:** Amsterdam 8, Zeeland 4, and one
  delegate each from Enkhuizen, Hoorn, Delft, and Rotterdam, with the
  seventeenth seat rotating. Source:
  https://www.encyclopedia.com/history/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/heeren-xvii

- **Reading B (four charter blocs), HIGH:** Amsterdam 8, Zeeland 4, de Maaze
  (Rotterdam + Delft) 2, Noord-Holland (Enkhuizen + Hoorn) 2; the seventeenth
  rotates among Zeeland, Maas, and Noord-Holland by majority vote — the
  individual-city seat assignment is an editorial inference, not the charter's
  language. Source: https://nl.wikisource.org/wiki/Octrooi_van_de_VOC

(The arithmetic is identical in both readings — four small-chamber seats — so
the disagreement is over labelling, not seat counts. A minor transcription note:
the article numbering "II" comes from Valentijn's 18th-century edition and was
not in the original 1602 manuscript, but the Dutch text is consistent across
sources regardless of numbering.)

The board's mandate to direct expeditions is stated in the next article
(Article III): the College meets "om te resolveren, wanneer men zal equiperen,
met hoeveel schepen, waar men die zal zenden, ende andere dingen, den handel
betreffende" — to resolve when to fit out, with how many ships, where to send
them, and other trade matters. **[HIGH** —
https://nl.wikisource.org/wiki/Octrooi_van_de_VOC **]**

## 3. Annual goal-setting in the Republic: the Heren XVII autumn resolutions

Each autumn the Heren XVII fixed the year's expedition scale under a **single
agenda item**: "the number of personnel, ships, cash, trade goods and provisions
that shall be sent to Batavia by the respective Chambers." The resolution
typically opened by stating the headcount (e.g., on 22 August 1658 the meeting
resolved to send 3,970 heads east, three-fifths sailors and two-fifths
soldiers), then listed every ship to be sent with a specification of the
personnel each would carry. **[HIGH** — quoted verbatim from
https://vocwarfare.net/thesis/3/supplies **]**

The autumn session is also where the Republic answered Batavia's standing
demands. The authoritative Nationaal Archief introduction sets out the agenda
items: the quantity of goods to be sent to Asia (answering the **eis der
behoeften**, the order for supplies from the Governor-General and Council); the
quantity of gold, silver, and copper coin (answering the **eis der contanten**,
the order for ready money); the definitive list of ships; and the compilation of
the **eis van retouren** (the directors' own list of products they wished
returned). **[HIGH** —
https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/sites/default/files/afbeeldingen/toegangen/NL-HaNA_1.04.02_introduction-VOC.pdf **]**

### The two-stage order process, and which document it applies to

The Heren XVII did run a **two-stage provisional-then-definitive process**:
"Usually a provisional list was drawn up first; the definitive eis (order) was
only decided upon after the autumn auctions had been held," because the
directors waited on their own and the London sales figures before settling
quantities. **[HIGH** —
https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/sites/default/files/afbeeldingen/toegangen/NL-HaNA_1.04.02_introduction-VOC.pdf **]**

The second half of the contested claim below is confirmed verbatim: with regard
to the **Generale Eis** (Batavia's shopping list), "the meeting usually limited
itself to stating that the request 'had been approved and would be fulfilled,'"
sometimes with a note bringing down some requested amounts. **[HIGH** —
https://vocwarfare.net/thesis/3/supplies **]**

### Contested: which instrument the two-stage process belongs to

The original research claim fused two distinct demand documents that flow in
**opposite directions**, and attached the two-stage process to the wrong one.

- **Reading A (original), MEDIUM:** The two-stage provisional/definitive process
  applied to "the annual cargo demand" broadly, pairing an "eis van retouren"
  with a "definitieve eis." Source (for the confirmed half):
  https://vocwarfare.net/thesis/3/supplies

- **Reading B (corrected referent), HIGH:** The two-stage process belongs
  specifically to the **eis van retouren** — the directors' list of goods they
  wished to receive **FROM** Asia with the next return fleet — settled after the
  autumn auctions. The **Generale Eis / eis der behoeften** (Batavia's list of
  goods to be sent **TO** Batavia) was handled as a single item the autumn
  meeting simply approved. These are separate instruments in opposite
  directions; conflating them is the error. Sources:
  https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/sites/default/files/afbeeldingen/toegangen/NL-HaNA_1.04.02_introduction-VOC.pdf
  and https://vocwarfare.net/thesis/3/decision-making

## 4. Fleet instructions issued by the Heren XVII

The Heren XVII designed the **instructies** (instructions) carried by outgoing
fleets, and those instructions were the vehicle for strategic objectives such as
where to fight and whom to damage. The content tracked the diplomatic moment.
Verified detail: at the **autumn 1661 meeting** the directors, anticipating peace
with Portugal, designed new instructions for the outgoing fleets that **struck
out** all the clauses about inflicting as much damage as possible on the
Portuguese — while, off the resolution-record, separately commissioning and
dispatching war fleets to Ceylon (1661) and Mozambique (1662). **[HIGH** —
https://vocwarfare.net/thesis/3/communication **]**

> **Kill-and-replace.** A draft claim asserted the reverse — that the 1661
> instructions *included* clauses about damaging the Portuguese, plus "secret
> orders sent to the Cape for incoming fleets." The source says the opposite
> (the clauses were struck), and contains no Cape/incoming-fleet element; its
> covert traffic ran to Batavia (via the *Nieuwenhove*) and to Ceylon. The
> original is recorded in `../validation/refuted.md`; the corrected reading above
> is asserted in its place.

The directors did keep **secret resolutions** in separate books, which is where
the most sensitive war orders could live, but these survive only for the late
18th century, so their content for the 1602–1700 period cannot be reconstructed.
**[HIGH** — https://vocwarfare.net/thesis/3/communication **]**

## 5. Goal-setting in Asia: the High Government and its demand documents

In **1609** the directors decided to place supreme command in Asia in the hands
of a **Governor-General assisted by a Raad van Indië (Council of the Indies)**.
After Batavia was founded in **1619**, it became the seat of the Governor-General
and Council — soon designated the **Supreme Government (Hoge Regering)** and the
highest VOC authority in Asia. **[HIGH** —
https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/sites/default/files/afbeeldingen/toegangen/NL-HaNA_1.04.02_introduction-VOC.pdf **]**

It was the job of the Governor-General and Council to compile the **generale eis
van Indië** (general order from Asia), "in which the amount of monies, goods,
ships and crews considered necessary for the business overseas was summed up. In
the sessions of the Heren XVII it served as a guideline for decision-making on
this subject. The orders from the various establishments were included in the
generale eis; the Governor-General and Council were empowered either to reduce or
increase each order." **[HIGH** — quoted verbatim from
https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/sites/default/files/afbeeldingen/toegangen/NL-HaNA_1.04.02_introduction-VOC.pdf
. Note: this passage was originally mis-cited to bgb.huygens.knaw.nl, which does
not contain it; the correct source is the Nationaal Archief introduction.**]**

So the demand documents that carried Asian expedition needs back to the Republic
were the **eis der behoeften** (supplies), the **eis der contanten** (ready
money), and the aggregating **generale eis van Indië** — and the Republic's
autumn resolutions were the answer to them.

### Contested: the size of the Council of the Indies

- **Reading A (nine ordinary members from 1617), MEDIUM:** The Raad van Indië
  from 1617 comprised nine regular members. Source (per the verification
  ledger's rival citation): the Brill/TANAP Niemejer chapter on the Hoge
  Regering archive, https://brill.com/display/book/9789047421795/Bej.9789004163652.1-556_008.xml
  *(not independently fetched in this run; cited as reported).*

- **Reading B (six councillors besides the Governor-General), HIGH:** The
  authoritative Nationaal Archief introduction states that "eventually the aim
  was to have six councillors besides the Governor-General in office in Batavia,
  while there would also be a few extraordinary councillors, who would enjoy only
  an advisory capacity" — i.e., roughly seven full members, not nine, with the
  number varying because of frequent vacancies. Source:
  https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/sites/default/files/afbeeldingen/toegangen/NL-HaNA_1.04.02_introduction-VOC.pdf

The two figures are in genuine tension and the body's size in fact fluctuated, so
the membership number is left unsettled here. The surrounding facts — 1609
formation, Governor-General + Raad van Indië structure, Batavia as seat from
1619, highest authority in Asia, and its issuing of instructions to ships'
commanders (scheepsgezagvoerders) — are confirmed and not in dispute. **[HIGH]**

## 6. The principal report document: the Generale Missiven

The **Generale Missiven** were the annual letters "waarin Gouverneur-Generaal en
Raden ... aan de Heren Zeventien een algemeen verslag gaven van het bedrijf in
Azië en Zuid-Afrika" — in which the Governor-General and Council gave the Heren
XVII a general account of operations in Asia and South Africa. They also
"vatten ... de berichten samen die ze uit de vestigingen ontvangen hadden"
(summarised the reports received from the settlements), giving the directors
"doorlopende informatie" (continuous information) about the VOC's settlement
areas. **[HIGH** — quoted verbatim from
https://resources.huygens.knaw.nl/vocgeneralemissiven ; independently called
"the most important means of communication between patria and Batavia" at
https://vocwarfare.net/thesis/3/communication **]**

This is the relay document by which Batavia kept the Heren XVII informed of Asian
conditions and forwarded strategic intelligence upward, closing the loop with the
demand documents and the directors' instructions.

## 7. Document types in the surviving VOC archive

The VOC's goal-setting and reporting generated a range of document types that
survive (in part) in the Nationaal Archief collection 1.04.02 — among them the
**missiven** (letters), **daghregisters**, **resoluties**, journals,
**instructies** (instructions to staff and to local functionaries), the **eisen**
(eis der behoeften / eis der contanten / eis van retouren), treaties, and the
Batavia–chamber correspondence. **[HIGH** —
https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/sites/default/files/afbeeldingen/toegangen/NL-HaNA_1.04.02_introduction-VOC.pdf **]**

### The "full range" framing is wrong in three specific ways

A draft claim asserted the archive holds the "full range" of VOC document types
including daghregisters, **bills of lading**, **price lists**, and **profit-loss
statements**. The authoritative introduction contradicts this:

- **Profit-loss statements never existed.** "No profit and loss account was made
  up." They cannot be in the archive. **[HIGH]**

- **Daghregisters are largely gone, not a standing holding.** Of the five
  important categories of records forwarded from the establishments, two "have
  been almost entirely lost ... the dagregisters (diaries) of the different
  establishments and the registers of the accountants stationed in Asia." **[HIGH]**

- **Bills of lading** (cognossementen) are not named anywhere in the 125-page
  introduction as an archived series, and the "full range" framing is further
  undercut by documented mass destruction — some 9,500–10,000 volumes, mainly
  17th-century, sold to the highest bidder in the winter of 1821/1822. **[HIGH]**

All three corrections are from
https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/sites/default/files/afbeeldingen/toegangen/NL-HaNA_1.04.02_introduction-VOC.pdf .
The remaining document types in the list (missiven, daghregisters as a category
that *existed*, resoluties, journals, instructies, eisen, treaties, Batavia–
chamber correspondence) are confirmed.

### Contested: the "three-category factory records" schema

A neat three-category schema — **missiven** (reports from a factory head to the
Governor-General and Council in Batavia), **daghregisters** (kept by factors),
and **instructies** (to new factors) — is genuinely useful but is not a general
characterisation of the whole VOC archive.

- **Reading A (general archive taxonomy), MEDIUM:** The VOC archive is organised
  into these three factory-level categories. Stated source:
  https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/netherlands-nn-archives/ *(returned
  HTTP 403; could not be read directly).*

- **Reading B (case-specific origin), HIGH:** The exact three-category
  formulation comes from Stephan van Galen, "The Dutch-Arakanese Relationship,
  1608–1683," where it describes the **Arakan factory's** records specifically
  ("the missiven, or reports sent by the head of the Dutch factory in Arakan to
  the Governor and Council in Batavia ... the daghregisters held by the factors
  ... the instructies to new factors"), not the VOC archive as a whole. Source:
  https://groups.google.com/g/soc.culture.burma/c/9C12G7tEkn0

The three record types all genuinely existed at factory level (the introduction
confirms dagregisters, outgoing missiven from establishments, and instructions to
local functionaries), so the substance is defensible; what is contested is
whether the tidy three-part schema is a general taxonomy or a single case study.

### Contested: what the Batavia Daghregister principally was

- **Reading A (letter register, not a chronicle), MEDIUM:** The Daghregister van
  Batavia (1624–1806) was, in the 17th century, principally created to register
  political advisories and news reports from VOC establishments — "literally
  thousands of incoming and outgoing letters" — and "was not intended to be a
  written chronicle of events in the first place"; duty clerks selected and
  registered the information daily. Source:
  https://sejarah-nusantara.anri.go.id/daily_journals/
  *(URL returned HTTP 403 / DEAD on fetch; content verified via search-engine
  cache only.)*

- **Reading B (a diary kept by the castle secretary), MEDIUM:** The Nationaal
  Archief VOC introduction translates *dagregister* as "diary" and treats the
  Batavia register as a diary kept by the castle secretary — a chronicle of daily
  events for which incoming letters were source material. Source:
  https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/sites/default/files/afbeeldingen/toegangen/NL-HaNA_1.04.02_introduction-VOC.pdf

The two readings may simply reflect two document uses sharing the name: a
letter-registry function at Batavia versus an event/activity diary at ship or
factory level. Left ambiguous at MEDIUM. The uncontested facts — the 1624–1806
span and the heavy letter-registration role — hold either way.

### The Batavia Resolutions (confirmed, distinct from the Daghregister)

The **Resoluties of Batavia Castle** are the **authorised final decisions** of
the Supreme Government (Hoge Regering) of the VOC in Asia, bearing the **original
holograph signatures** of the serving Governor-General and Council. All ~211,000
folio pages of the original minutes (1613–1810) have been digitised and
published. They are an administratively **distinct series from the Daghregister**
and constitute the formal decisions of the Hoge Regering. **[HIGH** —
https://sejarah-nusantara.anri.go.id/generalresolutions/ ; digitisation of all
330–331 volumes corroborated at
https://www.cortsfoundation.org/news/105-july-2015-resolution-books-batavia-online
. The "211,000 pages" vs. "232,000 scans" difference is a units discrepancy
(folio pages vs. scan files), not a contradiction.**]**

## 8. Officer-level expedition records (the Banda case, 1621)

By the 1620s, fleet and garrison commanders reported their own actions and
objectives directly to the Heren XVII. A concrete, archivally-anchored example is
Jan Pieterszoon Coen's dispatch on the Banda campaign, **dated 6 May 1621**
(Nationaal Archief 1.04.02, inv. 1073) — the bronnenbox source citation confirms
"verslag van de gebeurtenissen op Banda 6 mei 1621 ... inv.nr. 1073," matching
the document named in the research question. **[HIGH** —
https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/beleven/onderwijs/bronnenbox/hoe-ging-het-verder-op-banda **]**

> **Sub-question 2(a) — own losses in the hill engagement.** Translating the
> dispatch text presented in the bronnenbox: "In deze ontmoeting kregen wij 35
> gewonden en 9 doden, waaronder kapitein De Ros met zijn vaandeldrager" — in
> this engagement we had **35 wounded and 9 dead, among them Captain De Ros with
> his standard-bearer (ensign)**. Coen had sent a force of 80 musketeers and two
> companies of 70 men each up the mountain; the enemy followed his retreat along
> a narrow ridge and the disordered sailors nearly broke. **[HIGH** —
> https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/beleven/onderwijs/bronnenbox/hoe-ging-het-verder-op-banda **]**

(A note on cross-source variance: some English summaries give 6 dead / 27 wounded
for the *overall* Lontor island campaign, and the 7 March probing action returned
1 dead / 4 wounded. The 9-dead / 35-wounded figure is the one tied specifically
to the hill fight in this 6 May dispatch.)

### Contested: what the ~1,200 figure refers to

The dispatch reports a figure of roughly 1,200 — and the very source that
presents the text disagrees with itself about its meaning, so this is genuinely
ambiguous.

- **Reading A (≈1,200 captured / taken alive), MEDIUM:** The dispatch text reads
  "In totaal hebben wij omtrent 1200 zielen gekregen" — in total we have **taken
  about 1,200 souls** — with "gekregen" (taken/obtained) pointing to people seized
  alive; the same passage adds that the ship *'t Postpaert* "gaat mee om de
  gevangenen te helpen bewaren" (goes along to help guard the **prisoners**),
  reinforcing that these were captives subsequently deported. Source:
  https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/beleven/onderwijs/bronnenbox/hoe-ging-het-verder-op-banda

- **Reading B (≈1,200 died), MEDIUM:** The same bronnenbox page carries an
  editorial gloss immediately after the figure — "(red.: zijn circa 1200 man
  overleden)" — reading it as approximately **1,200 people who died.** Source:
  https://www.nationaalarchief.nl/beleven/onderwijs/bronnenbox/hoe-ging-het-verder-op-banda

The ambiguity is internal to the authoritative source (the text says "taken," the
editor's note says "died") and is compounded in secondary literature by
conflation with the much larger starvation death toll Coen reported separately.
The figure is therefore presented both ways and not settled here. What is not in
dispute: the dispatch also records that 45 orangkajas (Bandanese notables) were
re-arrested from the ship *Dragon*, and that "verscheidene andere" (several
others) were killed beyond the 1,200. **[MEDIUM]**
