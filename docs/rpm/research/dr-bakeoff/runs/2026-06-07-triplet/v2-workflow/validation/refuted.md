# Killed and replaced claims — reasons and drop tally

This file records every load-bearing claim the adversarial panel either **killed** (removed, no rival asserted) or **replaced** (original removed, a better-supported rival asserted in the report). Claims that merely carried a caveat but were still asserted are not repeated here. References use the verification-ledger claim order; in-report cross-references use the R-numbers below.

## Replaced claims (original killed, rival reading asserted in report)

**R-1 — "bij toerbeurte" / "original 17th-century Dutch wording" (ledger claim 3).**
Killed original: that the NL Wikisource octrooi contains the original 1602 wording and uses the phrase "bij toerbeurte."
Reason: the surviving text reads **"by beurten"** (confirmed at fetched artifact 01, line 549, and cross-confirmed at vocsite, artifact 05). The page's own closing note (artifact 01, line 639) states the modernised clause text is François Valentyn's 18th-century rendering (*Oud en Nieuw Oost-Indiën*, deel 1, pp. 186–191), not the 1602 manuscript.
Asserted instead: rotation phrase "by beurten van die van Zeeland, Maaze ende Noordholland … by de meeste stemmen," with the Valentyn-provenance caveat.

**R-2 — autumn-meeting accountability resolutions (ledger claim 15).**
Killed original: that the **autumn** meeting passed "resolution after resolution obliging the Chambers to account for their artillery stocks and material sent."
Reason: the VOC Warfare decision-making page (fetched artifact 15) places that exact passage in the **spring** meeting paragraph. The autumn meeting decided ship/soldier/armament numbers and the Generale Eis; the spring meeting did the reckoning and accountability.
Asserted instead: autumn = directives; spring = accountability + shipbuilding specification. (Report §2, "Correction to a common framing.")

**R-3 — the ~1,200 Bandanese "died" (editorial gloss).**
Killed original: the Nationaal Archief page's parenthetical "(red.: zijn circa 1200 man overleden)" — that ~1,200 men died.
Reason: Coen's own surrounding sentences (fetched artifact 07) describe the 1,200 "zielen" as living **prisoners** sent on the *Dragon* to Jakarta and guarded by the *Postpaert*, "tot Jakarta voor de handel gebruikt te worden" (to be used at Jakarta for trade). The gloss contradicts the dispatch text.
Asserted instead: ~1,200 = Bandanese captives shipped to Batavia as enslaved trade labour. (Report §3b.)

**R-4 — Van Goens 21 Sep 1650 "instructie" to Siam (ledger claim, Cambridge Itinerario).**
Killed original: that Van Goens received a formal *instructie* from Batavia on 21 Sep 1650 defining his Siam diplomatic/trade objectives.
Reason: the document at that date is an **akte van aanstelling** (appointment deed) for Van Goens as commissioner / directing chief of the Siam comptoir (NA 1.10.32, Van Goens family papers). The only *instructie* in the article's references is "Instructie voor Westerwolt ende Lieversz, 24 mei 1650" (VOC 1180), addressed to different officers. 21 Sep 1650 documents in the article are outgoing missives to Siamese officials.
Asserted instead: nothing — the specific Van Goens-instructie pin is not asserted in the report.

**R-5 — Van Lodensteijn / King of Macassar instructie example (ledger claim, Wikipedia van der Hagen).**
Killed original: that the Heren XVII issued an instructie to Commander Van Lodensteijn to **support** the King of Macassar against Spanish/Portuguese threats.
Reason: the named Wikipedia source covers only Van der Hagen's sealed instructions and never mentions Van Lodensteijn. The actual archival text (NL-HaNA 1.04.02 inv. 1111, pp. 411/553) shows the order was issued by the **Governor-General and Council at Batavia** (not the Heren XVII) and directed the fleet to **attack and blockade Macassar** — the opposite of supporting its king.
Asserted instead: nothing — example dropped.

**R-6 — 1650 "generale instructie" = three trade categories at offices (ledger claim, UMass PDF).**
Killed original: that the 1650 generale instructie "distinguished three categories of trade at different offices," establishing a standing framework for outbound fleets.
Reason: the UMass PDF contains no such passage. The real document (NA VOC introduction, Gaastra) classifies VOC **establishments** by the legal/political basis of Dutch presence (conquest / exclusive contract / equal-footing treaty) — not "trade at offices," and says nothing about outbound fleets.
Asserted instead: nothing — claim dropped as framed.

**R-7 — Council of the Indies "nine ordinary members from 1617" (ledger claim 23).**
Killed-strength original: stated at the level needed to assert exactly nine ordinary members.
Reason: contested. Multiple Dutch sources (Dutch Wikipedia Raad van Indië; vocsite; tacotichelaar.nl) give the ordinary membership as **usually six**. The stated ANRI source returned HTTP 403. Meeting frequency "2–3×/week" also unconfirmed (one source says "almost daily").
Asserted instead: establishment 1609–10 and Batavia Castle seat retained as MEDIUM; exact council size not asserted. (Report §5, flagged.)

**R-8 — Generale Missiven "thirteen volumes, 1610–1761" (ledger claim 28).**
Killed-strength original: the fixed volume count and 1761 end-date.
Reason: outdated — a fourteenth volume (Deel XIV, 1761–1767, RGP Grote Serie 270, Sidestone Press) exists. The Tasman "summarized in the missive of 12 December 1642" pin is also unverified (Tasman departed Aug 1642, returned mid-1643).
Asserted instead: series + function (annual Batavia → Heren XVII reporting, RGP, from 1610) as MEDIUM; volume count and Tasman-date not asserted. (Report §5, flagged.)

**R-9 — De Vlamingh van Oudshoorn "Governor of the Moluccas" (ledger claim 30).**
Killed original: his title as "Governor of the Moluccas," and the factsanddetails.com source.
Reason: the named source contained none of the claimed content. Authoritative sources (Atlas of Mutual Heritage portrait inscription; Livinus Bor, *Amboinse Oorlogen* title page) give his operative title as **superintendent of the eastern districts** (with "governor of Amboina" as the related role).
Asserted instead: Hongi mechanics, Ternate etymology ("armada"), 1651–1656 dates, and the corrected title (superintendent of the eastern districts). (Report §5.)

**R-10 — Coen dispatch Colenbrander "pp. 625–679" (ledger claim 6).**
Killed-strength original: the page range pp. 625–679 in Bescheiden I for the 6 May 1621 dispatch.
Reason: the Internet Archive full text shows the 6 May 1621 dispatch begins at p. 625 but the next letter (15 Nov 1621) begins at p. 640 — pp. 641–679 belong to a later dispatch. The range conflates multiple letters.
Asserted instead: inv. 1073 + 6 May 1621 + Colenbrander as the standard transcription (vol. 1 on archive.org); specific page range not asserted.

## Killed claims (no rival asserted — recorded only here)

**K-1 — English Wikisource "Translation:VOC charter" as the source for the seat-allocation article (ledger claims 1, 2).**
Reason: that page is a ~3,000-byte stub containing only the preamble; it contains no numbered articles and no board-composition text. It cannot support the seat-allocation or rotation claim. The numbers and rule are asserted in the report only from the Dutch original (artifact 01) and vocsite (artifact 05).

**K-2 — Instructie as a "distinct issuing document type" with sealed-orders-after-departure as a named class (ledger claim 16, /supplies pin).**
Reason: the cited /supplies page contains none of this; the 1661 Portuguese-clause detail is on /communication and concerns a different meeting. Neither page treats "Instructie" as a formal named document class or describes sealed delivery. Not asserted.

**K-3 — Artikelbrief as a goal-assigning document issued by the VOC chambers (ledger claim, Dalhousie thesis).**
Reason: the word "artikelbrief" does not appear in the 113-page thesis at the named URL. Independent Dutch sources attribute the artikelbrief to the **States-General**, not the VOC chambers, and it is a conduct/discipline code rather than an expedition-goal document. Not asserted as part of the goal-document taxonomy.

**K-4 — "Commission for Secret Affairs" as a named Heren XVII commission class (ledger claim 20).**
Reason: the named NA introduction PDF lists a nameless "secret committee" among wartime Heren XVII commissions but contains no entity called "Commission for Secret Affairs"; that phrase appears only in a Ceylon context elsewhere. The core document classes (Resoluties, Haags Besogne, colony letters, post-1700 accounting/musters) are asserted; the named secret-commission is not.
